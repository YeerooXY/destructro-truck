# First-playable truck physics

Instantiate `preload("res://src/gameplay/truck.gd").new()`. The node extends `RigidBody2D`, creates one rectangular `BodyCollision`, draws cosmetic wheels, and adds presentation-only `FollowCamera`. Physics does not import world, UI, scoring, progression, saves, or network modules.

Public calls:

- `configure(overrides) -> bool`: validated partial tuning snapshot, accepted before launch only. `get_tuning()` returns a frozen copy. Dimensions, mass, centre of mass, friction, damping, speed, bounce, energy, rotation, and stopping limits are configurable.
- `reset_run(run_id, spawn = Vector2(160, 520))`: clears commands, event IDs, consumed sources, resources, velocity, contacts, held controls, and camera. It preserves whether simulation was enabled. The next launch synchronizes its captured start transform through the direct physics state, avoiding stale server transforms during immediate restarts.
- `set_simulation_enabled(enabled)`: freezes physics and the launch cursor/run clock; clears held control when disabled. Queued events remain pending until resume, including a launch paused before its first physics tick. Linear/angular velocity survives freezing and resumes through the direct state callback.
- `accept_command(command) -> bool`: validated contract command, monotonically increasing sequence/time. One `launch` tap commits the cycling timing cursor. `rotate` stores the held axis; `nudge` stores direction/strength until an explicit zero command. Rotation consumes no resource. Nudge is an acceleration integrated against active physics time, with partial final energy use.
- `accept_contact(contact) -> bool`: publishes observation only; intact building/aerial/pickup IDs are once per run, surfaces have a short observation cooldown. The world decides destruction and rewards.
- `request_resources(request, reason = "building") -> bool`: queues a bounded contract speed cost, then velocity-change impulse, then bounded resource refill. Each source ID is reserved once per run, immediately on acceptance. Accepted reasons: `building`, `target`, `pickup`, `equipment`.
- `get_snapshot()`: deeply read-only contract snapshot of current state, including viability. `get_launch_feedback()` separately returns `{ready, phase, accuracy}`; `phase` is the cursor position from 0 to 1, centre is best. `is_viable()` is an observation, never a run result.

Signals are `gameplay_event(event)` and `snapshot_published(snapshot)`. Events carry a local sequence; the run coordinator must assign the one global run sequence and active time when combining producers. Events are flushed outside the physics callback so consumers may safely defer world changes. Snapshots are signalled at 12 Hz by default, while the read-only current observation updates each physics step.

Ground/wreck `StaticBody2D` nodes use collision layer 1, mask 2, metadata `object_id` and `contact_kind` (`ground` or `wreck`). Truck uses layer 2, mask 1, shape-cast continuous collision detection. Missing classifications become `unknown`; they do not grant bounce/recharge rewards. World intact buildings and aerial targets can be `Area2D` and relay contract contacts and requests. Contact normals use Godot's world-space body normal. Speed and request impulse units are px/s, positive Y is down. Only this module changes its body's velocity.

The custom force integrator retains Godot collision solving, applies bounded gravity/drag, caps rotation and linear speed, and uses distinct energy-losing ground/wreck bounce restitution. Tiny resting impacts never produce a bounce. Clean upright landings restore a small bounded amount of nudge energy. Remaining recovery energy grants two seconds of extra stall reaction time; unused energy cannot prevent an eventual stop forever.

The isolated laboratory is `tests/scenes/gameplay/physics_lab.tscn`: Space launch, left/right rotate, WASD nudge, R reset. `tests/scenes/gameplay/physics_regression.gd` runs real engine launch, collisions, speed cost, nudge depletion, pause preserving velocity, ten restarts, 18 seconds of capped-impulse/rotation stress, an immediate restart after spinning, inverted landing, and a 70-second unattended simulation. `tests/unit/gameplay/test_physics.gd` covers deterministic extreme bounds, cost direction, restitution, partial nudge, long rotation, dwell, malformed requests, and 100 local resets.

Verified with Godot 4.7.2 on Windows using `--headless --path . --script res://tests/test_runner.gd`, `--headless --path . --script res://tests/scenes/gameplay/physics_regression.gd --fixed-fps 60`, and `--headless --path . --script res://tests/integration/test_playable.gd --fixed-fps 60`; all pass. The isolated regression's unattended inverted attempt now stops once around 8.12 seconds with nine events and 97 signalled snapshots. Its 60 Hz physics steps run faster than real time for repeatable automation. The lab scene also loads independently without script errors. Runtime physics writes use Godot's [direct-state integration callback](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_physicsdirectbodystate2d.html).

The initial `physics_debug_v1` prototype uses friction 0.18 and linear damping 0.045. An earlier trial (0.025 / 0.012) let the integrated steering policy coast through empty terrain until 56.55 seconds, despite its last interaction at 8.55 seconds. The selected values give the same legal policy eight buildings and three aerial targets, 7,179 px of distance, and a stop at 14.25 seconds after its last interaction at 10.83 seconds. An unassisted accurate launch still reaches five buildings and two targets in 10.42 seconds. The development-only `tuning_probe.gd` compares the earlier and selected values plus a continuing nudge-recovery policy; it introduces no runtime finish condition.

These are debug-yard feel values, not accepted final balance. Stock launches are 800–1120 px/s at 20 degrees upward; speed caps are 1600 px/s total and 700 px/s upward. The short yard now favors frequent attempts with decisions over passive travel after the authored opportunities. The requested eventual 5–8-minute Survival economy, upgraded launch-angle challenge, final equipment, accessibility settings wiring, and human acceptance of handling still require later content/progression integration and playtesting. No final art or independent wheel/suspension simulation is included.
